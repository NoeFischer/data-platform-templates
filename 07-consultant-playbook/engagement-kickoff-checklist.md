# Engagement Kickoff Checklist

> Your personal checklist for the first 1-2 weeks of a new engagement. The stuff you always mean to do but forget by the third project because you're already heads-down in the work. Print it, tape it to your monitor, and don't skip the "soft" items — those are the ones that matter most.

## Before day 1

- [ ] Contract / SOW reviewed — understand what you're actually accountable for
- [ ] Key contacts identified (sponsor, day-to-day lead, technical counterpart)
- [ ] Access requests submitted (email, Slack/Teams, repos, cloud accounts, VPN)
- [ ] Read any existing documentation they've shared (even if it's outdated — that tells you something too)
- [ ] Prep your "first meeting" questions (see meeting prep template)
- [ ] Set up your project folder structure (copy this repo)

## Week 1: Learn before you build

### Understand the people

- [ ] Meet the sponsor 1:1 — ask: "What does success look like to you personally?"
- [ ] Meet the project lead 1:1 — ask: "What's been tried before? What went wrong?"
- [ ] Meet the technical team — ask: "What are you most worried about?"
- [ ] Identify the unofficial influencers — who do people actually listen to?
- [ ] Understand the org chart vs. how decisions actually get made (these are different)
- [ ] Ask: "Who will resist this project and why?" — someone will; find out early

### Understand the work

- [ ] Get a walkthrough of the current data landscape (sources, tools, flows)
- [ ] Ask to see the existing dashboards / reports people actually use
- [ ] Ask: "What question can you not answer today that you wish you could?"
- [ ] Understand the data team's current skill set and capacity
- [ ] Review any prior attempts, failed projects, or existing platforms
- [ ] Find out what the procurement / security review process looks like (this always takes longer than you think)

### Set up your ways of working

- [ ] Agree on communication channels (where do you chat, where do you email)
- [ ] Set up recurring meetings (standups, stakeholder check-ins, steering)
- [ ] Agree on how decisions get documented (point them to the decision log)
- [ ] Set up a shared workspace for deliverables
- [ ] Clarify your working hours and availability expectations

## Week 2: Establish the foundation

- [ ] Draft the project charter and get it reviewed
- [ ] Run the priorities alignment canvas with key stakeholders
- [ ] Complete the stakeholder analysis (even if just for yourself)
- [ ] Draft a rough timeline / milestone plan
- [ ] Identify the first quick win — something you can deliver in 2-4 weeks that builds credibility
- [ ] Write up your initial findings and share them (shows you're listening)

## Personal reminders

- [ ] Don't start solutioning in week 1. Listen first.
- [ ] Don't promise timelines until you understand the environment
- [ ] Take notes in every meeting — you'll forget who said what by week 3
- [ ] Find the person who's been there longest. Buy them coffee. They know everything.
- [ ] Your first impression is your strongest leverage. Use it to set expectations about scope discipline, not to be a people-pleaser.
